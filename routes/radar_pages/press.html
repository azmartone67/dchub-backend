The world's busiest data-center hub is out of power — and the line for more is four years long.

On a single July night, one Virginia county pulled more electricity than the entire state of New York. Ask its grid for more, and you'll wait until roughly 2030. Here's why the story of AI is really a story about the wires — told from live data, not a press release.

DC Hub Intelligence· The Dispatch· A grid briefing for the press desk

Ashburn, Virginia may be the most consequential place in the digital world that most people have never heard of. The unassuming exurb west of Washington routes an enormous share of the internet through more than 170 data centers — and on a July night in 2026, they drew {{ashburn_load_mw}} megawatts from the grid, more electricity than the whole of New York State was using at the same hour.

Then comes the twist. A developer who wants to build the next data center here, and asks the grid for the power to run it, will wait roughly fifty months to be connected. The busiest grid in America is, for practical purposes, full — and the gap between where the computing wants to sit and where the electricity can actually arrive has quietly become the defining infrastructure story of the decade. The live data shows exactly where it breaks, and where it is beginning to open up.

What to watch

Three threads for the next twelve months

Thread 01

The wind belt's moment

SPP and MISO curtail double-digit and mid-single-digit shares of clean power while clearing queues in about two years. Watch for data centers that stop chasing the coasts and start co-locating with spilled wind.

Thread 02

Texas's enormous bet

ERCOT is sitting on {{ercot_queue_gw}} GW of queued power, roughly 225 GW of it large-load driven by data centers. The open question isn't ambition — it's whether the grid can actually energize it, and how fast.

Thread 03

Virginia's 2030 cliff

With a fifty-month queue, new greenfield capacity in the world's #1 market won't arrive until the decade's end. Expect the growth to shift to retrofits, backfill, and the frontier grids — and expect the politics to get loud.
